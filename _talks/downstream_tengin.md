### Coming Soon

This research will be presented at the **Borneo International Conference on Marine and Environmental Science (BICAME)** on September 16, 2026.

Reservoir operation can alter downstream flow conditions and influence river morphology through changes in erosion, deposition, and sediment transport. This study evaluates the short-term morphological response of the Tengin River downstream of the Sepaku Semoi Dam under different operational discharge conditions.

A two-dimensional hydro-morphodynamic model was developed using Delft3D to simulate flow hydraulics, sediment transport, and bed-level evolution over a representative 35-day discharge period. The study compares observed reservoir inflow and downstream release regimes and incorporates representative upstream suspended sediment concentration based on probabilistic analysis of field measurements.

The results indicate that discharge regulation changes the spatial distribution of erosion and deposition but produces only minor changes in overall channel morphology. In contrast, the inclusion of upstream suspended sediment promotes widespread channel infilling and produces greater reductions in effective flow area. These findings highlight the stronger influence of sediment availability on downstream morphological evolution and river conveyance capacity compared with discharge regulation alone.

The presentation will discuss the implications of these findings for morphological monitoring, sediment management, adaptive engineering measures, and sustainable downstream river management.
